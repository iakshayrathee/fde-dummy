# 5-Minute Video Script (v5 — final)

**Challenge 1 — AI as the Driver's Manager**

**726 spoken words + 5s title + 12s rider clip = 4:58 at 155 wpm** (4:50 at 160). Read-off version:
[`04-teleprompter.md`](04-teleprompter.md). Companion leave-behind: [`07-one-pager.md`](07-one-pager.md).

**What changed from v4 — and why.** The judge's review scored v4 at 7.5, with *design imagination* stuck at 6.5
across four drafts. That was the real failure: the brief says **"forget how delivery apps work today"** and I kept
designing a *better* system instead of a *different* one. v5 rebalances hard —

- **The radical block is now 83 seconds, 28% of the video.** Seven things that don't exist today (§3 of the answer);
  four make the cut here. This is what was actually asked for.
- **A deeper reframe.** Not "the algorithm is a mute manager" but *which half* of management was automated: the gig
  economy automated allocation, scoring and punishment, and deleted coaching, advocacy and protection. **It kept the
  whip and threw away the hand.**
- **Engineering depth moved to the one-pager**, except the one line worth its seconds (*no write access*). A
  leave-behind after a customer meeting is itself an FDE behaviour.
- **New closing argument: they can't build this in-house.** Not for lack of engineers — because neutrality can't be
  built by the people who sign the paycheque.

## [1:07 – 1:32] THE REFRAME *(66 words)*

**Visual:** Two columns. **ALLOCATE · MEASURE · PUNISH** fills in green. **COACH · EXPLAIN · ADVOCATE · PROTECT**
stays empty, then greys out.

"He already *has* a manager. It's the dispatch algorithm — it sets his workload, scores him, pays him, can deactivate
him.

But look at which half of management got automated. A manager allocates, measures and punishes. A manager also
coaches, explains, advocates and protects. The gig economy automated the first half brilliantly and simply deleted
the second.

**It kept the whip and threw away the hand.**"

---

## [1:32 – 2:22] THE THREE THINGS *(129 words)*

**Visual:** **MEMORY · AUTHORITY · ACCOUNTABILITY.** On write access, show four boxes — `Payments` `OMS` `TMS`
`Customer` — with `WRITE` lighting up. On the scorecard, four metrics with `throughput` struck through.

"So I'm not adding a manager. I'm restoring the half that was thrown away. Three things do that.

**Memory.** He tells it once — that this gate is locked after eight. And it propagates: none of the other forty-nine
thousand ever hit that wall again.

**Authority.** And the engineering version of that word: the reason this doesn't exist isn't that the models weren't
good enough. It's that the agent has **no write access.** Waiving a penalty is a write to your payments system.
Authority, inside an enterprise, is an API scope and a spend limit.

**And accountability.** You can't make an agent loyal with a system prompt. So put loyalty in the scorecard —
earnings per hour, retention, whether he got promoted. Not throughput. **The agent's boss is the driver.**"

---

## [2:22 – 3:46] THE BRIEF SAID FORGET THE APP *(224 words — the heart of the video)*

**Visual:** Four cards, ~20s each. Keep them stark — one line of text, no diagrams. On the last one, animate 400 dots
becoming one.

"None of what follows is a better version of something in the app.

**He never logs in. Ever.** His voice is his identity — attendance, delivery confirmation, payments. Login is the
most literacy-hostile object in enterprise software. For a man who can't read the field label, *forgot password* is
the end of his workday.

**The shift is dead.** He doesn't accept a block of time — he says *I need fifteen hundred today, finishing by six*,
and the agent solves backwards and tells him the odds. He stops being scheduled and starts being served.

**Agents negotiate with each other** — Ravi's agent and Suresh's swap two stops directly, no ops involved, because both
drivers end up better off. A market of driver-aligned agents finds allocations central dispatch can't see.

**And the one that matters most.** Four hundred riders each lose eighteen minutes a day at the same warehouse.
Individually, not one has a complaint worth making, and nobody has ever seen the pattern. The agent does — so it files
one case, with data, for four hundred people.

Operational friction only — never pay rates. **That limit is what keeps it alive.**

**Fifty thousand powerless people, collectively legible for the first time.**"

## [3:46 – 4:01] THE HARD EDGE *(39 words)*

**Visual:** **"These platforms already punish. Silently."**

"An agent that only defends him is a lawyer, not a manager. These platforms already punish — silently. Orders dry up,
nobody says why, nothing to appeal. So punishment becomes **spoken, specific and appealable.** Termination stays
human. Always."

---

## [4:01 – 4:26] WHY YOU CAN'T BUILD THIS IN-HOUSE *(55 words)*

**Visual:** One word, full screen: **NEUTRALITY.**

"They'll ask why they can't build this in-house.

They can build the voice — voice is commoditising. What they can't build is **neutrality.** An in-house agent is the
company's agent by construction. No rider will believe the thing built by the people who pay him is on his side — and
he'd be right.

Neutrality is the product."

---

## [4:26 – 4:49] HOW I'D SHIP IT *(60 words)*

**Visual:** `Wks 0–2: ride pillion · 500 tickets` → `one wedge · 200 drivers` → `mouth ✓ · hands ✗ · memory ✗`

"Nobody flips fifty thousand drivers. Two weeks I design nothing — I ride pillion and read five hundred tickets. Which is
a compressed version of the two years you spent running a recruitment agency before any of this.

And to be clear: I've just described a three-year platform. **I'd ship one exception handler.** One wedge. And note
what I'm **not** rebuilding — you already have the mouth. This needs hands and a memory."

>
> Give it memory, authority, and a scorecard — and point it at them."

  [1:07]; *"the agent has no write access"* at [1:32]; the 400-riders block at [2:22]; the recruitment-agency line at
  [4:26]. Evidence, reframe, engineering, imagination, research.
- **[2:22] is the heart of the video.** If a section gets your best delivery, it's this one. If you rush anything,
  rush [1:32].
- **Marked cuts if you overrun:** the "agents negotiate" card at [2:22] (−50w, ~19s) — it's the most conceptual and
  the least emotional. Then the second sentence of [4:26].
- **Link the one-pager** in the video description or last frame. Leaving a written artifact after a pitch is what the
  job looks like.
- **Do not exceed 5:00.** Overrunning a stated limit is itself a signal about self-editing.
