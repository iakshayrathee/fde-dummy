# Rider interview kit

**This is the only thing in the whole submission I cannot do for you, and it's worth more than everything I can.**

Field instinct is 15% of the score and currently sits at 5/10 because every detail about "Ravi" is invented. Two
hours of your time takes it to 9+. Nothing else available to you has that return.

---

## The plan — 2 hours, 3 riders

**Where.** A dark-store cluster at a shift change: Blinkit / Zepto / Instamart, 2–4pm on a weekday (the lull — they
have time and they're bored). Or a Swiggy/Zomato pickup point outside a mall food court. Avoid 7–10pm; they're
earning and won't talk.

**Approach.** Buy chai. Say exactly this, in Hindi/Marathi/Kannada:

> "Bhaiya, I'm building something for delivery partners and I don't want to guess. Ten minutes, and I'll pay you for
> your time — what's the most irritating part of your day?"

**Pay them ₹200–300.** Non-negotiable. You're taking earning time, and paying converts you from a nuisance into
someone who values their input. It also makes the whole exercise honest.

**Recording.** Phone voice memo, in your shirt pocket. Ask first: *"Can I record? Only I will hear it unless you say
otherwise."* If you want to use the clip in the video, ask separately at the *end* — after they've warmed up — and
offer to blur or use audio only. If they hesitate at all, use audio only and don't name them.

---

## The eight questions

Ask in this order. **The first three are warm-up. The gold is in 4–7.**

1. How long have you been doing this? How many apps do you run?
2. Roughly how many drops on a normal day, and what do you make?
3. What time do you actually start and actually stop?

4. **"What's the most annoying part of your day that has nothing to do with traffic?"**
   *The single best question in this list. It excludes the obvious answer and forces the real one.*

5. **"When was the last time your payment was wrong? What did you do about it?"**
   *Listen for whether they even bothered. Learned helplessness here is the finding.*

6. **"When did you last call support? Did it help?"**
   *Ask them to walk through it minute by minute. The dead time is the product.*

7. **"Has your work ever slowed down without anyone telling you why?"**
   *This tests my §5 claim about silent algorithmic punishment. If they describe it unprompted, you have the single
   most valuable quote in the submission. If they don't, I'm wrong and you should say so in the video.*

8. "If you could change one thing about the app, what?"
   *Weakest question. Ask it last, expect a boring answer, don't lead with it.*

**Then shut up.** The goal is 80% them talking. Every time you're tempted to explain your idea, ask "why?" instead.

## What to listen for

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Unpaid waiting time and where it happens | Validates the wedge (§11) and the collective (§3.5) |
| Whether they gave up on a wrong payment | The learned-helplessness finding is stronger than any complaint |
| Whether work has silently dried up | Validates or kills §5 — the claim I'm least certain about |
| How they actually talk to their phone now | Do they use voice notes? Voice search? This tells you if §3.1 is real |
| What they'd want to *learn* | Tests §3.4. If several say "English," that's a finding worth 10 seconds of video |
| Anything that surprises you | **The most valuable output. If nothing surprised you, you didn't listen.** |

## How this changes the submission

**The clip.** Best 10–15 seconds go in the slot at `[0:53]`, before the reframe. Pick the moment with the most
*emotion*, not the most information — one man sounding tired about a locked gate beats a clear articulation of a
process problem.

**One number.** Say something like *"all three told me they lose 20 minutes a day at gates"* — a small primary
statistic beats a large secondary one. n=3 stated honestly is more credible than n=50,000 assumed.

**Say the sample size out loud.** *"I spoke to three riders — that's not research, it's a sanity check."* Naming your
own limitation is what a good FDE does, and it inoculates you against the obvious pushback.

**And if they contradict me: change the answer and say so on camera.** *"I assumed X. All three said Y, so I changed
this."* That is the single most hireable sentence available to you in this entire submission — it demonstrates the
exact loop the job consists of. It's worth more than any idea in `01-the-answer.md`, including the good ones.
