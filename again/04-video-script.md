# Shooting Script — "Design for Illiterate Workers"
### Hunar.AI Product Thinking Challenge #3 · Runtime 4:48 of 5:00

**Why this challenge:** Challenge #1 rewards imagination — it explicitly invites you
to speculate. #3 hands you a population and dares you to know something real about
them. For a company that ran a recruitment agency for two years before writing
software, demonstrating fieldwork instinct is the higher-signal choice. Full
reasoning in `00-pick-decision.md`.

**Delivery math — read this, it matters.** This script is **604 spoken words** at
**143 wpm**, which is 253 seconds of speech, plus 31 seconds of call audio and a
4-second silent card. **Total 4:48.** Margin to the cap: 12 seconds.

That word count is machine-counted, not estimated. A previous version claimed 618
words / 4:56 and actually ran 702 words / **5:35**. If you edit any line, re-count.

> **Do this before anything else:** read all eight narration blocks aloud against a
> stopwatch at the pace you actually intend to use. If you exceed 265 seconds, take
> the trims in §5 **now**, not after you've edited.

**Format:** the video is mostly something you *hear*. If the argument is that these
workers can't read, the deliverable should prove it. You appear on camera twice, for
about 45 seconds total. Everything else is your voice over cards and simulated call
audio.

| **V2** | You on camera, closing | ~32s |
| **C1–C24** | Cards | see §4 |

**Language check.** This assumes you can record credible Hindi. If not, swap to any
Indian language you actually speak — it's arguably better because it's authentic —
or use Hindi TTS for the AI voice. Do **not** record Hindi you aren't fluent in; an
evaluator in this domain will hear it instantly, and a video about respecting
workers' languages that mispronounces one is worse than one that doesn't try. Either
way, have a fluent speaker check the Devanagari below before you record.

**Visual rules, applied everywhere.** Dark background, white type, one idea per card,
type large enough to read on a phone. No logos. No icons. **No stock photos of
smiling workers** — that would undercut the entire argument. Every statistic carries
a small source line, bottom-left.

**Subtitles are burned in, not a sidecar track.** Both the Hindi and your English
narration. The evaluator may watch on mute, and hard subtitles are quietly on-theme
for a submission about people who can't read the interface.

### `0:20 – 0:45` — THE FRAME · 59 words

**SCREEN:** `0:20` **V1 — you on camera.** Medium close-up, chest up. Plain wall,

`0:38` cut away to **C2**, then `0:42` **C3** — your voice continues over both.

**SAY:**

> A hundred thousand workers, mostly migrant women.
>
> Sunita can't read. Nor can twenty thousand of her colleagues.
>
> Forty percent can't read English — that's translation. Twenty percent can't read

>

>

**DIRECTION:** Slow down and drop pitch on the two bolded pairs. They're the thesis.

### `0:45 – 1:10` — WHY THEY PAY · 60 words

**SCREEN:** `0:45` **C4** — three lines appearing one at a time, timed to your voice.
`1:05` **C5** — full screen, large type.

**SAY:**

> Why would a factory pay for this?
>
> Karnataka garment workers earn about thirteen thousand rupees a month — thirty
> percent below other industries. In April, Noida factory workers struck because
> payslips didn't match the hours they'd worked.
>
> Wage confusion isn't a UX problem. It's attrition, and it's production stoppage.
>
> **The whole system costs under one day of her wages, per year.**

**DIRECTION:** This beat exists because an earlier cut explained the design and never
said why anyone would fund it. Deliver it flatly, like a fact, not like a pitch.

**SAY:**

**DIRECTION:** Say the quoted question the way the AI would — slower, warmer, a

## 6. Build order

Front-loads the risky work so a failure costs you hours, not the deadline.

1. **Read-aloud timing test.** Before anything else.
2. **Cut A1 to exactly 20 seconds.** If the cold open doesn't land, nothing after it
   matters. Build it first and listen to it ten times.
3. **Record N1 in eight blocks**, three takes each. Standing up. Never one long take.
4. **Lay N1 against the ledger in §3**, block by block.
5. **Drop in A2 and A3.**
6. **Build cards to fit the audio that already exists** — never the reverse.
7. **Shoot V1 and V2 last**, once you know exactly how much time is left. Same
   framing both times.
8. **Subtitle. Budget 90 minutes.** Auto-caption reliably mangles rupee figures and
   Hindi transliteration — the exact words that matter here. Read every line.
9. **Export** MP4 / H.264 / 1080p / 16:9.

**Then check, by playing the exported file:** under 5:00 · cold open starts at 0:00
with no title · every statistic shows a source · subtitles on both languages with
rupee figures correct · watch once **muted** and confirm it still follows · watch
once **on a phone** and confirm the card type is readable.

Recording technique, mic setup, phone-call audio filtering and the full pre-submit
checklist are in `05-shot-audio-list.md`.
