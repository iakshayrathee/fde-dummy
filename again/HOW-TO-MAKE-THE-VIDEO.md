# How to actually make this video

Plain version. Read this one file. The others are reference material — you don't
need to read them to make the video.

**Total time needed: about 4 hours.** You have two days.

---

## The big idea, in one paragraph

You are making a 5-minute video where **you talk over slides**, and three times
during it, a **short fake phone call plays** in Hindi with English subtitles. You
appear on camera twice for about 20 seconds each. That's it. There is nothing else
to it.

---

## What the 5 minutes contains

Think of it as four ingredients:

| Ingredient | How much | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Fake phone calls** | 31 seconds total | 3 short clips. A robot voice talking to a worker in Hindi. |
| **Your voice** | ~4 minutes | You reading a script out loud. |
| **Slides** | 32 slides | Simple dark slides with big white text. |
| **You on camera** | ~45 seconds | Two clips of you talking to the camera. |

The video is basically: **slides on screen, your voice on top, with three phone
calls dropped in, and your face at the start and the end.**

---

## The easy way to build it (no video editing skills needed)

**Use PowerPoint.** Not CapCut, not DaVinci. PowerPoint can do this whole job.

Here's why it works: PowerPoint has a feature called **Record Slide Show**. You put
your slides in order, hit record, and talk through them. It records your voice on
each slide and remembers your timing. Then you click **Export → Create a Video** and
it gives you an MP4. Done.

You can also drop an audio file onto any slide (Insert → Audio) and set it to play
automatically. That's how the phone calls get in.

Google Slides works too but has no built-in recorder, so PowerPoint is easier.

## Step 1 — Get the three phone call clips (1 hour)

These are the most important part of the video, so do them first.

**What they are:** a robot voice speaking Hindi. The exact Hindi words are written
out in `04-video-script.md` — look for the boxes marked **AUDIO A1**, **AUDIO A2**,
**AUDIO A3**. Each has the Hindi in Devanagari, plus the same thing written in
English letters so you can read it aloud even if you can't read Hindi script.

**How to get the audio — pick one:**

1. **You record it yourself** on your phone's voice recorder app. Best option if you
   speak Hindi. Read it flat and clear, like a machine, not dramatic.
2. **Use a free text-to-speech website.** Paste in the Hindi, pick a Hindi voice,
   download the MP3. Search "Hindi text to speech free" — several work fine. This is
   actually a *good* choice, because the caller in your story really is a machine.
3. **Ask a friend** who speaks Hindi to read it into their phone and WhatsApp it to
   you. Takes them two minutes.

**One line needs a second voice.** At the end of the first clip, the worker says
*"अच्छा… ठीक है"* (*"achha… theek hai"* — "oh, all right"). That's four syllables.
Any woman who speaks Hindi can record it in ten seconds. If you can't find anyone,
use a different text-to-speech voice.

**Important:** if you don't speak Hindi well, **don't fake it.** Either use
text-to-speech, or switch the whole thing to a language you do speak — Tamil,
Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, Kannada, anything. The point is that it's *her* language,
not that it's specifically Hindi. If you switch, just change one line in your script
from "migrant women" to name that state's workers.

**Save the three files as:** `call1.mp3`, `call2.mp3`, `call3.mp3`

## Step 2 — Make the slides (1 hour)

Open PowerPoint. You need **32 slides**. Most are one sentence in big white text and
take 90 seconds each.

**The look — same on every slide:**
- Background: black or very dark grey
- Text: white, big — 40pt or more
- Font: anything clean (Arial, Calibri, Helvetica)
- One idea per slide
- No photos of workers, no clipart, no logos

**On any slide with a statistic**, put the source in tiny text bottom-left — "NIST
FRVT", "NBER 2022", etc. Sources are listed at the bottom of `06-one-pager.md`. Two
minutes of work, and it makes the whole thing look researched.

### Slide 16 — the chart (spend ten extra minutes here)

Six vertical bars of different heights, each labelled as a group of workers. One
dotted horizontal line across all of them labelled **"average — 6%"**. Make one bar
much taller than the rest, colour it red, label it **"this group — 31%"**. Caption
underneath: **Never the average.**

You can draw this with six rectangles and a dotted line. It doesn't need to be a real
chart. This single image makes your entire argument about measurement in two seconds
without a word, and it's the slide people will remember.

### Slide 19 — the two bars

Two vertical bars side by side. The taller one labelled `₹13,100`, the shorter one
`₹12,480`. Draw a bracket between the tops and label it **₹620 less**.

## Step 3 — Attach the phone calls (15 min)

Three slides get audio: **slide 1, slide 17, slide 29.**

**How:** click the slide → Insert → Audio → Audio on My PC → pick your file. A speaker
icon appears. Click it, go to the **Playback** tab, set **Start: Automatically**, and
tick **Hide During Show**.

**Slide 1 is special** because the 20-second call has to run across slides 1 to 5
while the subtitles change. In the Playback tab, tick **Play Across Slides**. Then set
slides 1–5 to advance automatically — Transitions tab → untick "On Mouse Click", tick
**After**, and enter roughly: `4s / 5s / 6s / 4s / 2s`. Play it once and nudge those
numbers until the subtitles match the voice.

Slides 17 and 29 are single slides, so the audio just plays and you move on.

## Step 4 — Record your voice over the slides (1 hour)

The words you say are in `04-video-script.md` section 2, in the boxes marked
**SAY:**. There are eight of them. Read them in order. That's the entire script —
604 words.

**Before you record: read it out loud once with a stopwatch.** All eight SAY blocks,
start to finish. You should land around **4 minutes 15 seconds**. If you're over
4:30, you're talking too fast to be clear, or you need the trims listed in the
script's section 5.

**Then record:**

1. In PowerPoint: **Slide Show → Record Slide Show**
2. It goes full screen with a record button
3. Talk through your slides, clicking to advance as you go
4. **Record in chunks, not one long take.** Do a few slides, stop, check it, carry
   on. If you fluff a line, you can re-record just that slide — click on it and hit
   record again. You do not have to start over.

**Recording tips that actually matter:**
- Use earphones with a mic, or your phone's earbuds. Better than a laptop mic.
- Sit in a small room with soft stuff in it — a bedroom, not a kitchen. Echo is the
  only thing that sounds amateur.
- Stand up if you can. Your voice gets more authority.
- Speak slower than feels natural. Watch for the sentences in **bold** in the
  script — slow down on those and pause after. There are about eleven of them and
  they're the lines that count.

## Step 5 — Film yourself twice (30 min)

Two clips. Phone camera is fine. Prop it at eye level. Sit facing a window so light
falls on your face — never with the window behind you.

**Clip 1 — the opening (~15 seconds).** Say exactly this and then stop:

> A hundred thousand workers, mostly migrant women.
> Sunita can't read. Nor can twenty thousand of her colleagues.
> Forty percent can't read English — that's translation. Twenty percent can't read
> anything. Translation does nothing for them.

**Stop there.** The next two sentences ("So I inverted the default…" and "And one
rule holds it together…") are narrated over slides 7 and 8, not spoken on camera.

**Clip 2 — the closing (~38 seconds).** The whole CLOSE block, starting *"I wouldn't
build all five."* Say all of it on camera, right through to *"Make it useful to her
before it's useful to them."*

**Both clips must look identical** — same distance, same wall, same light. Film them
back to back so they match.

Then in PowerPoint: opening clip on **slide 6**, closing clip on **slide 31**. Insert
→ Video → This Device, set to play automatically, and drag it to fill the slide.

Those two slides need no narration — your voice is already inside the video.

## Step 6 — Export and add subtitles (1 hour)

**Export:** File → Export → Create a Video → Full HD (1080p) → Create Video. Save as
`Hunar-FDE-Challenge3-YourName.mp4`.

**Subtitles.** Your English narration needs subtitles too, not just the Hindi. The
easy way: upload the MP4 to CapCut (free, desktop or phone), use **Auto Captions**,
then **read every single line and fix it.** Auto-captions get rupee amounts wrong
constantly — "₹12,480" comes out as nonsense. Then export again.

If that feels like too much, upload to YouTube as **Unlisted**, let YouTube generate
captions, fix them in YouTube's editor, and share the link. Less polished but it
works and costs you 20 minutes.

Play the exported file and check:

- [ ] It starts immediately with the phone call — no title slide, no "Hi I'm…"
- [ ] Subtitles are there for both the Hindi and your own narration
- [ ] Rupee figures in the subtitles are correct
- [ ] Watch it once **on mute** — it should still make sense
- [ ] Watch it once **on your phone** — the text should be readable

Then send the video link plus the one-pager (`06-one-pager.md`, exported as PDF) by
**31 August, 9:00 AM IST.** Aim to send it by 7:00 AM so you have buffer.

## If you only have 2 hours

Cut this down without losing the argument:

1. **Skip filming yourself entirely.** Voice over slides for the whole thing. Costs
   you almost nothing.
2. **Use text-to-speech for all three phone calls.** Don't hunt for a second voice
   for Sunita's one line — just use a different TTS voice.
3. **Make 12 slides instead of 24** — keep the ones with the bold sentences and the
   numbers, drop the rest and hold slides longer.
4. **Still make slide 11** (the chart). It's the best thing in the deck.
5. **Use YouTube's auto-captions** and just fix the rupee figures.

A simple version done well beats an ambitious version done badly. The argument is
what's being judged, not your editing.
